# Times Magazine 2-Hour Creative Challenge

This is a (very small) website I tasked myself with creating for my Critical Theory for Media class, for our unit about Political Economy and Post-Colonial Theory.

Given that the assignment was to make something within the constraints of 2 hours, I was nervous about pulling off the zine format of the website, but I figured since I already plan to make my final project with a similar idea, I could use this assignment as an experiment while still adhering to the theme of the current chapter.

In today's political climate, I think this topic is extremely relevant, and I created this zine with news censorship and ignorance in mind. I recall a very recent time where there were riots in Paris, protests in parts of Mexico, and ongoing occupations in Gaza and the Congo, all while coverage of it goes mostly under the radar in US news cycles and social media. Instead, we're bombarded with red carpet appearances, big pop culture events, and constant distraction from outside issues. This project was mainly inspired by the specific and very recent day in which the Paris protests were taking place, while a few streets away, press for the upcoming HUNGER GAMES movie was happening at the same time, with actors and actresses dressed in lavish clothing and whatnot. Practically the exact plot of the Hunger Games itself, which I thought was ironic yet sad. 

In the chapter of the book, the author mentions how big businesses, corporations, and the government are keen for media outlets to "report on political and international affairs in a way that sympathizes with their government's point of view", or in other words, spinning the truth so that one party looks much better in an unfair situation. Most of the examples I included in my zine are US-related news articles placed next to their counterpart news to show the obvious difference in how the story is being told. In a situation where the US benefits, the way it is fed to Americans is vastly different to what may actually be going on in these respective countries. For example, with the Hunger Games analogy, to the US it might seem like all is well in Paris because we're seeing high-fashion and entertainment on our screens. Despite this, there are atrocities being committed by the government of France unto its people that is essentially being gatekept from us in small ways. Of course, we could find this information if we truly wanted to, but at least for me, until I had seen other people talking about it, I felt mostly in the dark about what was going on. Same for the protests going on in Mexico regarding a lack of clean water and the staggering missing persons situation, and much more in many other parts of the world.

The template application I used has a purposefully janky look to it, which is why the visuals might seem a little low-res or odd-looking. I quite like it, though, so I'm now definitely open to reusing the template for later projects!
